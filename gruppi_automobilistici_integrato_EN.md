## TL;DR

- **Capital ≠ vote in four key nodes**, confirmed on primary sources: **Porsche SE** holds **53.3% of the votes** but only **31.9% of the capital** of Volkswagen AG; **Lower Saxony** has **20.0% of VW votes** (statutory veto threshold via the *Volkswagen-Gesetz*, instead of the usual 25%) on a markedly lower capital stake; **Exor** has **15.48% of Stellantis capital** but **23.84% of the votes** (loyalty shares); **Renault** owns **35.71% of Nissan capital** but the voting rights remain contractually capped at **15%**.
- **The Renault-Nissan-Mitsubishi Alliance has been stable since 2024**: Renault's stake in Nissan (35.71% = 17.05% direct + 18.66% in a French trust, 693.1 million shares) is **unchanged since end-2024** and confirmed in the Q1/Q2 2025 press releases; no further sale from the trust appears executed as of July 2026. The Honda-Nissan merger **collapsed on 13 February 2025**. Nissan reduced its stake in Mitsubishi Motors from 34.07% to 24.05% with a buyback in November 2024.
- **Stellantis (Form 20-F 25 February 2026)**: Exor 15.48%/23.84% votes, EPF/Peugeot family 7.72%/11.89%, Bpifrance 6.64%/10.22%; Dongfeng reduced to just over 1%. No brand closed in the FaSTLAne 2030 plan (May 2026); Maserati stays a pure luxury brand, Lancia and DS become "specialty" brands.
- **Geely remains the main cross-cutting hub** of the graph (Volvo Cars 78.7%, smart 50% with Mercedes, Mercedes 9.69% capital, Aston Martin reduced to ~14% from the ~17% peak), but with stakes in continuous motion — to be verified at every update.
- **New cross-group edges that emerged only in Research B**, absent in A: VW→Rivian (15.9%, 50/50 JV "Rivian and Volkswagen Group Technologies"); Northvolt failure (VW ~21%) with assets passed to Lyten (also held by Stellantis).
- **Coverage extended to six new European groups (Research C, July 2026)**: Ferrari, AB Volvo/Volvo Group, Iveco Group, Bugatti Rimac, McLaren and Jaguar Land Rover were added to the graph. The most notable fact is that **Tata Motors** (India) is about to become the owner of both **Jaguar Land Rover** (since 2008) and **Iveco Group** (acquisition from Exor announced on 30 July 2025, close expected in the third quarter of 2026 but **not confirmed as completed at the end of July 2026**) — an interesting parallel to the dual Chinese presence already present in Mercedes-Benz/Volvo Cars/Stellantis.
- **Porsche AG has retreated from extra-core investments**: on 24 April 2026 it sold both its 45% stake in Bugatti Rimac and its 20.6% in Rimac Group to a consortium led by HOF Capital, closing a chapter of diversification that opened in 2021.
- **New layer of industrial partnerships (Research D, July 2026)**: connected to each other nodes already present but so far isolated — **Hyundai/Kia→Rimac Group (~12%)**, **VW→XPeng (~5%)**, **Exor→CNH Industrial (~26.9% cap./~42.5% votes**, the same loyalty-shares structure as Stellantis and Ferrari) and three industrial alliances **without** an equity exchange (**VW–Ford**, **BMW–Toyota**, **Stellantis–Toyota**). They confirm that the large European groups are also tied to non-European carmakers (Korea, China, USA, Japan) by technology agreements, not only by shareholdings.
- **European map completed with the independent carmakers (Research D, §15)**: added **Pagani** (founder), **Koenigsegg** (founder + **failed** relationship with NEVS/Evergrande), **Ineos Automotive** (INEOS/Ratcliffe), **Automobili Pininfarina** (Mahindra) and **Morgan** (Investindustrial). Also completed the shareholder base of **Aston Martin** with **Saudi PIF** (~16-18%) and **Mercedes-Benz** (<8%, technology partnership being diluted).

## Key Findings

1. **Never collapse capital and vote** in Porsche SE/VW, Lower Saxony/VW, Qatar/VW, Exor-EPF-Bpifrance/Stellantis, Renault/Nissan: in all these edges the voting figure is materially different from the capital figure and **must be represented with two distinct labels on the same edge**, not collapsed into a single number.
2. **The Renault-Nissan structure is more stable than Research A estimated**: it is not a process of continuous divestment, but a situation **frozen since end-2024** (35.71%/17.05%/18.66%), with only a change of accounting treatment (fair value through equity, June 2025, non-cash impact ~€9.3bn) and a declared but unexecuted intention by Nissan to go from 15% to 10% in Renault.
3. **Stellantis is fully consolidated and the FaSTLAne 2030 plan closes the uncertainty on the brands** that Research A flagged as dynamic: no brand closure/sale, Maserati confirmed pure luxury (with a detailed roadmap expected December 2026), Lancia and DS downgraded to "specialty" under Fiat and Citroën.
4. **Traton = commercial vehicles** (MAN, Scania, International Motors ex-Navistar since 1 October 2024, Volkswagen Truck & Bus Brazil); the **VW vans (Transporter/Crafter/Caddy) stay in the core Volkswagen brand**, not in Traton — a point confirmed by both pieces of research.
5. **Dual Chinese presence in Mercedes-Benz** (BAIC 9.98% votes, Geely/Tenaciou3 9.69% capital) remains a node of interest; the Kuwait Investment Authority stake should be updated to **~4.97%** (Research B), not ~6.8% as indicated in Research A (an outdated figure, already reduced by March 2023).
6. **Point unresolved by either piece of research**: the stake of Mitsubishi Corporation (trading house) in Mitsubishi Motors remains without a percentage verified on a primary source in both.
7. **Exor confirms itself as a multi-sector hub even outside Stellantis**: it holds 21.33%/32.32% (capital/vote) of **Ferrari** and 27.1%/43.1% of **Iveco Group** — the latter stake being sold to Tata Motors as announced in July 2025. The precise capital/vote breakdown for Ferrari, however, comes from a non-primary industry analysis, not an Exor/Ferrari filing.
8. **AB Volvo (trucks/buses/earth-moving machinery) remains an entity entirely distinct from Volvo Cars** (cars, controlled by Geely): the two companies separated in 1999. The graph must be updated carefully so as not to confuse the two nodes.
9. **Exor is the most pervasive holding in the graph after Geely**: besides Stellantis, Ferrari and Iveco Group, it also controls de facto **CNH Industrial** (~26.9% capital / ~42.5% votes), completing the map of the Agnelli group's industrial and agricultural vehicles. The control mechanism is always the same — loyalty/special voting shares that concentrate the vote well beyond the capital stake — replicated across four distinct entities.

## Details — by entity

### 1. Renault–Nissan–Mitsubishi Alliance

**Historical situation (pre-November 2023):** asymmetric — Renault held 43.4% of Nissan with full voting; Nissan held 15% of Renault without voting rights (French rule on cross-shareholdings, Code de commerce).

**8 November 2023 restructuring and current status (confirmed, unchanged since end-2024 — source: "Nissan's contribution" press releases Q1 2025 of 13/5/2025 and Q2 2025 of 30/7/2025):**
- **Renault → Nissan: 35.71% of total capital**, made up of:
  - **17.05% held directly** with full voting rights;
  - **18.66% transferred to an independent French trust** (693.1 million shares), voting neutralized for most decisions but economic benefit retained by Renault, ahead of a discretionary sale over time.
  - Divestment path from the trust (from the initial 28.4% to the current 18.66%): three sales — 211m shares (~5%, December 2023), 99.1m (~2.5%, March 2024), 195.5m (~5.0%, €494m, September 2024). **No further sale appears executed between mid-2025 and July 2026.**
  - 30 June 2025: change in the accounting treatment of the trust stake (to fair value through equity), with a non-cash loss impact of about €9.3bn and a Renault FY2025 group loss of about €10.9bn.
- **Nissan → Renault: 15% of capital, voting contractually capped at 15%.** The **31 March 2025** amendment lowered the binding minimum cross-shareholding threshold from 15% to 10% (explicitly leaving the Renault shares in the trust unchanged). Nissan CEO Ivan Espinosa declared (June 2025) the intention to go down to 10%, **but the sale does not appear executed as of July 2026.**
- ***[From Research A, not confirmed by B]*** The original November 2023 agreements also included a **Renault-Nissan commitment to invest in Ampere** (Renault's EV/software subsidiary); Renault withdrew Ampere's IPO in 2024, likely making this commitment obsolete. **Neither piece of research verified the current status of this clause: to be re-verified against the most recent Renault URD if relevant for the graph.**

**Nissan → Mitsubishi Motors:** reduced from **34.07% (506,620,577 shares) to 24.05%** through a ToSTNeT-3 buyback of 8 November 2024 (¥460.6/share, ~¥68.6bn cashed in), with **loss of automatic control over board appointments**. Mitsubishi Corporation (trading house, an entity distinct from the carmaker) keeps a residual stake **not precisely quantified on a primary source in either piece of research**.

**Honda-Nissan:** merger MoU of 23 December 2024, **collapsed on 13 February 2025** over Nissan's refusal to become a Honda subsidiary. Technological collaboration on batteries/software/EV continues (with Mitsubishi involvement); Foxconn remained interested in Nissan.

**Primary sources:** Renault Group, *Document d'enregistrement universel* 2024/2025 (AMF); Alliance/Renault "Nissan's contribution" press releases (2023-2025); Nissan Motor, *Yūkashōken hōkokusho* (EDINET); Mitsubishi Motors, buyback press release 7/11/2024.

**Edge classification:** cross-holding **asymmetric in capital (35.71% vs 15%) but equal in voting (15%/15%)**; historical control relationship Renault→Nissan (43.4%) **ended since November 2023**.

### 2. Porsche SE / Volkswagen AG / Porsche AG (data as of 31 December 2025)

VW share capital: €1,283,315,873.28; 295,089,818 ordinary voting shares.

- **Porsche SE → VW: 53.3% of votes / 31.9% of total capital.** The divergence stems from VW's dual capital structure (ordinary voting + listed preferred without votes); Porsche SE concentrates its grip on the ordinary shares.
- **Lower Saxony → VW: 20.0% of votes / ≈11.8% of total capital** (Research A estimate, not precisely re-checked by B but consistent). **Statutory veto** power via the *Volkswagen-Gesetz*, which sets the blocking threshold at 20% of votes instead of the usual 25% of German company law.
- **Qatar Holding LLC → VW: 17.0% of votes** (third shareholder).
- **Free float: 9.7%** of the ordinary shares.
- **VW → Porsche AG: 75% minus one share** of the ordinary shares (indirectly via Porsche Holding Stuttgart GmbH).
- **Porsche SE → Porsche AG: 25% plus one share** of the ordinary shares (≈12.5% of total capital). The preferred shares (P911) are listed in Frankfurt; the public holds only capital without votes.
- The **Porsche and Piëch** families control 100% of the ordinary shares of Porsche SE.

**Primary sources:** Volkswagen Group, *Annual Report 2025* — Shareholder structure; Porsche AG, Shareholder structure (IR); Porsche Automobil Holding SE, *Geschäftsbericht*.

**Classification:** Porsche SE **de facto control through a majority of votes** over VW (capital minority); VW **majority control** over Porsche AG; Porsche SE **direct blocking minority** in Porsche AG; Lower Saxony **minority with statutory veto power**.

### 3. Volkswagen Group / Traton SE and brands

- ***[From Research A, not re-checked by B]*** **Volkswagen AG → Traton SE: ≈90% of capital**, the rest free float. *To be re-verified against Traton's latest Annual Report, not confirmed with a primary source in Research B.*
- **Traton structure:** MAN (Germany), Scania (Sweden), **International Motors** (ex-Navistar, USA — rename effective **1 October 2024**, date confirmed by Research B), Volkswagen Truck & Bus (Brazil/South America), RIO services brand.

**Primary sources:** Volkswagen Group, *Annual Report 2025*; Traton SE, *Annual Report*; Transport Topics (25/9/2024) on the Navistar→International Motors rename.

**Classification:** VW **majority control** over Traton (~90%, to be re-confirmed); **full ownership (100%)** of Ducati; SEAT/CUPRA a VW subsidiary with CUPRA as an internal sub-brand.

### 4. Stellantis N.V. (Form 20-F as of 25 February 2026)

- **Origin:** equal PSA + FCA merger completed on 16-17 January 2021. **PSA and FCA no longer exist as independent/listed legal entities.**
- **Shareholding (capital / vote, due to the loyalty voting shares — additional vote after 3 years of continuous registered holding):**
  - **Exor N.V.: 15.48% of capital / 23.84% of votes** (449,410,092 ordinary shares + an equal number of Class A special voting shares).
  - **EPF — Peugeot family, via Peugeot Invest and Peugeot 1810: 7.72% / 11.89%.**
  - **Bpifrance Participations: 6.64% / 10.22%.**
  - **Dongfeng Motor: reduced to just over 1%** (it was 1.58% after the November 2023 buyback).
  - Free float: the rest on the market.
  - *Note:* compared with the 2024 Form 20-F (data as of 25/2/2025), the values were slightly different (e.g. Exor 23.89% votes) — annual micro-variations to be re-confirmed with each new 20-F.
- **Stellantis → Zhejiang Leapmotor: ≈21% of capital** (initial acquisition ~20% for €1.5bn, October 2023). **Leapmotor International B.V.: JV 51% Stellantis / 49% Leapmotor.** FAW acquired a **5% of Leapmotor** for CN¥3.74bn in 2025.
- **Governance:** **Antonio Filosa** CEO (appointed 28 May 2025, full powers from 23 June 2025), succeeding **Carlos Tavares** (left December 2024). John Elkann remains Executive Chairman.
- **Brands — FaSTLAne 2030 plan (€60bn, Investor Day 21-22 May 2026):** **no brand closed or sold.** Four global brands (Jeep, Ram, Peugeot, Fiat); five regional (Chrysler, Dodge, Citroën, Opel, Alfa Romeo); **DS and Lancia** managed as "specialty" brands under Citroën and Fiat respectively; **Maserati** confirmed a pure luxury brand (detailed roadmap expected December 2026). FY2025 results (26/2/2026): net loss ≈€22.3bn, driven by a "reset" of ≈€22.2bn announced on 6/2/2026.

**Primary sources:** Stellantis N.V., *Annual Report and Form 20-F for the year ended December 31, 2025* (25/2/2026); Stellantis-Leapmotor press release (8/5/2026); Automotive World on the Leapmotor JV.

**Classification:** Exor **top shareholder, de facto control reinforced by voting (not absolute majority)**; Leapmotor **significant minority stake (~21%)** + **Stellantis-controlled JV (51%)**.

### 9. AB Volvo / Volvo Group

- **Not to be confused with Volvo Cars:** AB Volvo (trucks, buses, construction equipment) and Volvo Cars (cars, a Geely subsidiary) are **distinct entities since 1999**, when Ford bought the car division from AB Volvo.
- **Dual-class share structure:** A shares (one vote) and B shares (one tenth of a vote), which concentrates decision-making power relative to capital in the hands of Swedish institutional investors.
- **AB Industrivärden: ≈9.6% of capital**, indicated as the largest single shareholder (source: aggregator champsignal.com, data as of 20/12/2025). The official Volvo Group Investor Relations shareholding page was located but the dynamic shareholder table was not extractable with a static fetch in this research.
- **Not verified:** the precise split of voting rights of Industrivärden and of the Wallenberg sphere/Investor AB, both traditionally central to the governance of Swedish groups, was not confirmed on a primary source.
- **Geographic breakdown of capital** (MarketScreener): Sweden ≈27.6-31.5%, China ≈5.6%, Norway ≈0.9-1.9%, USA ≈0.5% — a figure aggregated by country, not a single identified shareholder.
- **Historical link with Renault Trucks:** Renault Veicoli Industriali (RVI) was sold to Volvo Group in 2001; Renault SA received a stake of about 15% in Volvo Group in exchange, gradually divested in the following years. The Renault Trucks brand keeps the name but no longer has any equity link with Renault Group.
- **Brands:** Volvo Trucks, Mack Trucks (USA, since 2000), Volvo Buses, Renault Trucks.

**Sources:** Volvo Group, IR page *Ownership information* (page structure extracted, dynamic table not captured); champsignal.com (20/12/2025); MarketScreener, geographic breakdown of Volvo Group shareholding; industry historical note on the RVI/Volvo transaction of 2001.

**Classification:** AB Industrivärden **top minority shareholder (secondary figure)**; Renault Trucks **historical relationship ended with Renault Group, now a 100% Volvo Group subsidiary**.

### 12. McLaren Group / McLaren Automotive / McLaren Racing

### 14. Additional industrial partnerships and cross-shareholdings (Research D)

> This section comes from **Research D** (July 2026) and connects entities already present in the graph with edges not yet mapped. No data comes from a direct primary filing: all the stakes are secondary (financial press and corporate press releases) or established industry knowledge, and should be treated with a lower degree of certainty than Research B.

**Minority equity investments:**
- **Hyundai Motor Group → Rimac Group (~12%):** in 2019 Hyundai Motor and Kia jointly invested about 80 million euros in the Croatian maker of hypercars and EV technology. The combined stake (~12%) survived the 2021 reorganization that created Bugatti Rimac. Exact split not re-verified on a primary source.
- **Volkswagen → XPeng (~5%):** Volkswagen invested about 700 million dollars in July 2023 for a minority stake (~5%) in the Chinese electric vehicle maker, with the goal of co-developing electric platforms for the Chinese market.
- **Exor → CNH Industrial (~26.9% capital / ~42.5% votes):** Exor is the de facto controlling shareholder of CNH Industrial (agricultural and construction machinery, Case IH and New Holland brands) thanks to special voting shares — the **same loyalty-shares structure** that concentrates the Agnelli family's voting power in Stellantis and Ferrari. CNH is the company **from which Iveco Group was spun off in January 2022**: its inclusion completes the map of the Exor group's industrial vehicles.

**Alliances and cooperations without an equity exchange:**
- **Volkswagen ↔ Ford:** industrial alliance without cross-holding. Ford builds the electric **Explorer** and **Capri** on Volkswagen's **MEB** platform and the two groups share the **Ranger** and **Amarok** pickups. The **Argo AI** autonomous-driving JV was closed in 2022 and since then the cooperation has narrowed to the already-started agreements.
- **BMW ↔ Toyota:** cooperation without an equity exchange. Shared sports platform (**BMW Z4** and **Toyota Supra**) and joint development of **hydrogen fuel-cell** systems, relaunched in 2024-2025.
- **Stellantis ↔ Toyota:** supply agreement without an equity exchange. The **Toyota Proace** and **Proace City** vans are rebadged versions of Stellantis commercial vehicles (former PSA platform), with shared production in Europe.

**Sources:** Hyundai Motor Group press releases and industry press on the 2019 Rimac investment; financial press on Volkswagen's investment in XPeng (July 2023); Volkswagen/Ford and BMW/Toyota press releases on their respective alliances; Stellantis (ex PSA)/Toyota press releases on the commercial vehicle supply; Exor N.V. and CNH Industrial shareholding documents.

**Classification:** Hyundai Motor Group, Volkswagen (→XPeng) and Exor (→CNH) **minority stakes** (Exor→CNH with capital/vote divergence); Volkswagen↔Ford, BMW↔Toyota and Stellantis↔Toyota **alliances or industrial cooperations without cross-holding** — a category visually distinct from the alliance *with* cross-holding of the Renault-Nissan type.

### 15. Independent and niche European carmakers + full Aston Martin shareholder base (Research D, extension)

> This section completes the European map with the independent carmakers not tied to the eight large groups and with the Aston Martin shareholders not yet represented. As in §14, the data are secondary or established industry knowledge; many of these companies are unlisted, so the stakes are orders of magnitude and not filing-grade values.

**Independent and niche carmakers (with ownership):**
- **Pagani** (Italy, hypercars): wholly controlled by founder **Horacio Pagani** and family. No external shareholders.
- **Koenigsegg** (Sweden, hypercars): majority control by founder **Christian von Koenigsegg**. In 2019 **NEVS** (National Electric Vehicle Sweden, **Evergrande** group) invested about $150m for **20%** plus a JV for an affordable EV; the relationship has **effectively lapsed** with the collapse of Evergrande (2022-2024) — represented as an ended relationship, with NEVS as a failed entity.
- **Ineos Automotive** (United Kingdom, Grenadier off-roader): a **100%** division of Sir Jim Ratcliffe's **INEOS** chemical group; builds at Hambach (France), the ex-Smart plant bought from Mercedes in 2020.
- **Automobili Pininfarina** (Germany, Battista hyper-EV): **100%** controlled by the Indian group **Mahindra**, distinct from the design house Pininfarina S.p.A. (also ~76% Mahindra).
- **Morgan** (United Kingdom, artisanal): majority held by the Italian private equity firm **Investindustrial** since 2019; the Morgan family and employees keep a minority. Investindustrial was **formerly an Aston Martin shareholder** (2013-2020, ~37.5%), then exited with the arrival of Yew Tree — a historical ended edge that connects Morgan to the rest of the graph.

**Full Aston Martin shareholder base** (besides Yew Tree 31% and Geely ~14% already present):
- **Saudi PIF (Public Investment Fund): ~16-18%**, second-largest shareholder since 2022, with board seats. Stake potentially varied by the numerous capital increases.
- **Mercedes-Benz: <8%**, being diluted from a maximum once meant to reach 20%. Derives from the technology partnership (AMG engines, electric/electronic architecture) in exchange for shares.

**Sources:** established industry notes on Pagani/Koenigsegg/Ineos/Pininfarina/Morgan (largely unlisted companies); financial press on the Koenigsegg-NEVS deal (2019) and the collapse of Evergrande; financial press on Saudi PIF's entry into Aston Martin (2022) and the dilution of the Mercedes stake.

**Classification:** Horacio Pagani and Christian von Koenigsegg **full/majority control** of their respective brands; INEOS→Ineos Automotive and Mahindra→Automobili Pininfarina **full ownership (100%)**; Investindustrial→Morgan **majority control**; NEVS→Koenigsegg and Investindustrial→Aston Martin **historical ended relationships**; Saudi PIF and Mercedes-Benz→Aston Martin **minority stakes**.
